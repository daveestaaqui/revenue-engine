# Surplus Docket: Institutional Authority & Digital PR Blueprint

## 1. Positioning

**Core position**

> Surplus Docket is a court-record intelligence platform for law firms evaluating surplus recovery matters—with traceable source records, structured diligence, and attorney-controlled review.

**Strategic objective:** Become a trusted reference for how surplus data should be documented, evaluated, and transferred into legal workflows.

Institutional authority should come from a published methodology and independent scrutiny—not from describing the platform as an official court system or an already-adopted industry standard.

**Suggested homepage headline**

> Court-source surplus intelligence. Built for attorney review.

**Supporting message**

> Evaluate potential matters against source records, screen for senior-lien issues, and move reviewed records into your intake workflow.

*Publish feature claims only after confirming actual functionality and coverage.*

### Messaging guardrails

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| “Official court surplus database” | “Court-source surplus data,” with coverage and source disclosures |
| “Guaranteed recoverable funds” | “Potential surplus matters requiring entitlement and priority review” |
| “Automated senior lien elimination” | “Automated senior-lien screening and exclusion of flagged records from qualified lead views,” if supported |
| “Tyler-compliant recovery platform” | “Research and workflows informed by post-*Tyler* property-rights developments” |
| “Court-certified deadlines” | “Rule-referenced deadline calculations, subject to attorney verification” |
| “Native Clio/Filevine integration” when only CSV exists | “Clio/Filevine-formatted CSV export,” where tested and supported |
| “The institutional data standard” | “A published surplus-data quality specification for law-firm review” |

**Important distinction:** Software can filter records for lien risk. It does not extinguish senior liens, conclusively establish priority, or determine a claimant’s entitlement.

## 2. Build Assets Worth Citing

### A. Court Surplus Data Quality Specification

Publish a versioned, freely accessible specification covering:

- Court, jurisdiction, case number, proceeding type, and source URL or document identifier.
- Document date, retrieval timestamp, and last verification date.
- Reported surplus amount and the source supporting it.
- Separation of tax foreclosure, mortgage foreclosure, execution sale, and other proceeding types.
- Party identities and documented ownership or succession relationships.
- Lien findings, search scope, source dates, unresolved issues, and screening status.
- Applicable rule citations, triggering events, and assumptions for calculated deadlines.
- Clear distinction between extracted facts, inferred fields, and attorney conclusions.
- Corrections, disputed records, and historical revisions.

**Editorial value:** Gives publications and practitioners something concrete to evaluate and cite.

**Governance:** Identify authors and reviewers, solicit outside comments, and publish a change log. Do not imply bar, court, or independent standards-body adoption.

### B. Post-*Tyler* Research Hub

Anchor the hub in *Tyler v. Hennepin County*, 598 U.S. 631 (2023).

Explain that the Supreme Court recognized a takings claim concerning government retention of tax-sale value exceeding the tax debt. Do not portray the decision as a universal recovery procedure, automatic entitlement, or elimination of state-law requirements.

Create separately reviewed FL, TX, GA, CA, NC, and TN pages addressing:

- Proceeding types and governing authorities.
- Claimant eligibility and competing interests.
- Filing procedures, service requirements, and relevant deadlines.
- Probate, successor, assignment, and priority issues.
- Unresolved questions and the date of legal review.

**Editorial value:** A careful state-specific resource is more useful than a broad “millions owed” narrative.

### C. Surplus Data Reliability Report

Use a disclosed methodology and aggregate findings:

- Missing or inconsistent case identifiers.
- Stale balance information.
- Duplicate records.
- Unresolved ownership or lien issues.
- Source-to-platform update delays.

Do not publish claimant contact details, sensationalized personal stories, or “recoverable dollars” estimates unsupported by entitlement review.

### D. Attorney Diligence Checklist

Create an ungated checklist covering provenance, ownership, liens, probate, notice, deadlines, and intake documentation.

**Primary citation destination:** The checklist or methodology page—not a sales landing page.

## 4. White-Hat Editorial Pitch Angles

### Angle 1: Post-*Tyler* Rights Need Reliable Procedures

**Headline:**  
“After *Tyler*, Better Surplus Recovery Starts With Better Records”

**Thesis:** Property-rights developments do not remove the need to establish ownership, priority, timeliness, and the correct procedural route.

**Evidence package:** Primary authorities, a state-specific process map, and an attorney-reviewed example.

### Angle 2: A Transparent Alternative to Opaque Finder Practices

**Headline:**  
“What a Surplus Recovery Record Should Disclose Before Anyone Signs”

**Thesis:** Source transparency, understandable fees, documented conflicts checks, and informed client choice offer a stronger model than pressure-based solicitation.

Criticize documented practices—not all nonlawyer assistance providers as a class. Finder and assignment rules differ by state. Attorney involvement is not itself proof of compliant conduct.

### Angle 3: Lien Screening Is Not Lien Extinguishment

**Headline:**  
“Why a Surplus Balance Is Not a Recoverable-Value Estimate”

**Thesis:** Responsible platforms distinguish reported funds from lien findings, unresolved priority questions, and attorney conclusions.

**Evidence package:** Screening methodology, coverage gaps, and anonymized exception cases.

### Angle 4: Deadline Automation Requires an Audit Trail

**Headline:**  
“A Legal Deadline Without a Rule Citation Is Just a Date”

**Thesis:** Useful calculations expose the governing rule, triggering event, service assumptions, holiday treatment, and version history.

Federal Rules of Civil Procedure calculations belong only in applicable federal civil proceedings. State surplus matters may instead be governed by statutes, state rules, local rules, and case-specific orders. Not every deadline is extended by ordinary weekend or holiday rules.

### Angle 5: Provenance Belongs in Intake

**Headline:**  
“Preserving the Court Record When a Lead Becomes a Matter”

**Thesis:** Intake transfers should preserve case identifiers, source references, unresolved issues, and review status—not merely names and amounts.

## 5. Digital PR Execution Rules

- Lead with original research or a practitioner resource.
- Tailor each pitch to a specific publication and audience.
- Offer a methodology briefing or written answers; never condition access on a backlink.
- Seek contextual citations, not exact-match keyword anchors.
- Disclose paid placements and use appropriate sponsored-link treatment.
- Avoid private blog networks, bulk directory submissions, link exchanges, and fabricated reviews.
- Do not use court or bar logos without permission.
- Publish author credentials, coverage limits, review dates, and corrections.
- Minimize personal data in examples; public availability does not make every republication appropriate.

## 6. 90-Day Rollout

### Days 1–30: Establish the Evidence Base

- Audit product claims and existing site language.
- Publish the data specification, methodology, corrections process, and coverage matrix.
- Release the diligence checklist and one attorney-reviewed state guide.
- Prepare screenshots or demonstrations substantiating each marketed capability.
- Build an initial list of 30 qualified institutional prospects.

### Days 31–60: Earn Initial Distribution

- Submit accurate profiles to relevant directories.
- Send 8–12 individually researched editorial pitches.
- Propose two CLE programs with qualified faculty.
- Seek independent review of the published specification.
- Launch practice-group-specific outreach to supported jurisdictions.

### Days 61–90: Expand What Works

- Publish additional reviewed state guides.
- Release a methodology-backed reliability report.
- Pursue editorial follow-ups where there is genuine interest.
- Update messaging based on attorney objections and product-usage evidence.
- Publish corrections and specification revisions openly.

## 7. Measurement

**Authority**
- Relevant editorial referring domains.
- Independent citations to methodology and legal resources.
- Accepted educational contributions.
- Quality of expert review and feedback.

**Commercial**
- Qualified law-firm visits.
- Self-serve trial starts.
- First source-record inspection.
- First completed screening review.
- First successful intake export.
- Trial-to-paid conversion by practice group and jurisdiction.

**Trust**
- Record correction turnaround.
- Percentage of records with complete provenance.
- Percentage of calculated deadlines with citations and assumptions.
- Screening exceptions and false-positive/false-negative findings.

**Avoid:** Treating domain authority, raw backlink count, or total database value as the primary measure of success.
