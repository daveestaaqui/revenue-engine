2. First-Principles Kinetic Discovery

Why Conventional Dry Lab Ovens Stall — and How Two-Zone Fixes It

Coupled thermodynamic & evaporation modeling revealed a hidden wet-bulb bottleneck that explains why dry-oven bench drawdowns under-cure—and how a simple bench steam pan resolves it.

1. The ~51°C Evaporative-Cooling Trap

In a dry forced-air oven (pH2O ≈ 0.02 atm), rapid evaporation cools the wet coating to ~51°C regardless of dwell time. Ammonia stays trapped and the film dries solid at only ~10–16% modeled crosslinking.

2. Humidified Zone 1 Unlocks 6.7× Higher Cure

Raising Zone 1 water vapor pressure (≥ 0.15 atm; ~0.35–0.45 atm target) elevates wet-film temperature to ~80–85°C while keeping liquid water present—driving ~69% cure on production dryers and ~75–90% in lab convection ovens.

3. Dry Zone 2 Seals Carnauba Above Tm (~84°C)

Once crosslinked, dry 135°C Zone 2 air finishes drying and melts the nonionic carnauba wax into a continuous moisture barrier that re-solidifies into non-tacky flakes for 52°C neutral OCC-E repulping.
