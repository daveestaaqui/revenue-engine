Tyler v. Hennepin County: The Ruling That Unlocked Billions in Recoverable Surplus

In Tyler v. Hennepin County, 598 U.S. 631 (2023), the Supreme Court ruled unanimously that governments violate the Takings Clause when they retain surplus equity from tax foreclosure sales exceeding the debt owed. The decision invalidated "home equity theft" statutes in at least 12 states and unlocked an estimated $8+ billion in retroactively recoverable surplus proceeds nationwide.

For asset recovery attorneys, this created the single largest expansion of claimable surplus inventory in decades. Counties that previously retained overages are now legally obligated to distribute them to former property owners and their heirs — but only if a claim is filed within the statutory window.

Frequently Asked Questions

Key legal and delivery details on our public records feed.

How does daily data delivery work?

Subscribers receive standardized CSV, XLSX, and JSON files every morning at 7:00 AM EST. Each feed includes verified case docket numbers, surplus balances, property addresses, and calculated statutory deadlines ready for CRM import.

How are Florida, Texas & Georgia statutory claims handled?

The feed tracks specific statutory rules: Florida (Fla. Stat. § 197.582, 120-day notice window, 20% cap benchmark), Texas (Tex. Tax Code § 34.04, 2-year limitation, 25% cap benchmark), and Georgia (O.C.G.A. § 48-4-5, 5-year claim window).

What makes Surplus Docket different from raw PDF lists?

Raw county lists are 70–80% encumbered by bank and senior mortgage liens. Surplus Docket algorithmically scrubs institutional liens so your team only targets pure individual owner and estate heir equity.
