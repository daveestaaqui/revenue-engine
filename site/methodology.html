Data Provenance & Institutional Lien Scrubbing Methodology

An engineering and legal-tech specification on how Surplus Docket ingests, cross-references, scrubs institutional encumbrances, and standardizes multi-state judicial surplus records.

1. The Operational Bottleneck in Public Records Scraping

Following real estate tax deed foreclosure auctions, county clerks and district court civil registries hold surplus funds created when winning auction bids exceed the opening statutory bid. While this information is public, raw court lists present severe operational hazards for legal practitioners:

Unfiltered Senior Liens: Many raw clerk rows represent properties with active senior mortgages (such as commercial banks or loan servicers) or municipal tax assessment liens. Under state statutory priority schemes, these institutional lienholders possess legal priority over the former titleholder.

When recovery firms rely on raw scraper lists, staff spend significant time contacting former owners, only to discover during title examination that the funds must be disbursed entirely to a senior mortgage servicer.

2. Surplus Docket's 4-Stage Cleansing Pipeline

Surplus Docket runs an automated daily pipeline to filter out corporate and senior encumbrances before records enter the feed:

1

Stage 1: Continuous Court Registry Ingestion

Aggregates fresh auction sale confirmations and registry deposits across all 6 core jurisdictions: Florida Circuit Courts (Fla. Stat. § 197.582), Texas District Court Civil Registries (Tex. Tax Code § 34.04), Georgia Superior Court & Sheriff Dockets (O.C.G.A. § 48-4-5), North Carolina Superior Courts (N.C.G.S. § 105-374), Tennessee Chancery Courts (T.C.A. § 67-5-2501), and California County Tax Collector Registries (Cal. Rev. & Tax Code § 4675). Ingests official docket numbers, parcel IDs, final auction bids, statutory minimums, and confirmed surplus balances.

2

Stage 2: Official Encumbrance & O.R. Cross-Referencing

Automated scripts cross-reference the Official Records (O.R.) and clerk judgment indexes for the parcel, examining recorded Lis Pendens, Certificates of Title, and tax collector final judgment decrees to identify all named defendant lienholders.

3

Stage 3: Institutional Entity Purge (Lien Scrubbing)

Our proprietary entity classifier identifies and removes corporate institutional claims (commercial banks, mortgage trusts, servicer loan pools, junior HOA assessment foreclosures). If recorded senior debt exceeds the surplus balance, the lead is suppressed from the feed.

4

Stage 4: Statutory Benchmark Tagging & Multi-Format Export

Surviving verified records are annotated with exact governing statutory references (Fla. Stat. § 197.582, Tex. Tax Code § 34.04, O.C.G.A. § 48-4-5, N.C.G.S. § 105-374, T.C.A. § 67-5-2501, and Cal. Rev. & Tax Code § 4675), court-holiday adjusted filing deadlines, and statutory fee benchmarks, exported in CSV, XLSX, and REST JSON daily at 7:00 AM EST.

3. Open Government & Public Records Provenance

All data collected and standardized by Surplus Docket is gathered strictly in compliance with state open records statutes:

Florida Sunshine Law
Fla. Const. art. I, § 24

Guarantees public access to all judicial and county comptroller tax deed ledger records.

Texas Public Info Act
Tex. Gov't Code § 552

Authorizes public inspection and extraction of district court civil excess proceeds registries.

Georgia Open Records
O.C.G.A. § 50-18-70

Governs public disclosure of tax commissioner and sheriff tax sale excess funds.

North Carolina Public Records
N.C.G.S. § 132-1

Guarantees public inspection of Superior Court tax foreclosure judgment dockets.

Tennessee Public Records
T.C.A. § 10-7-503

Mandates open access to Chancery Court Clerk & Master delinquent tax sale registries.

California CPRA
Cal. Gov. Code § 7920.000

Authorizes inspection of county tax collector auction trust excess proceeds ledgers.
